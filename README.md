# ***Welcome!***

Hi all, my name is Jake Chouinard! I am a Research Associate at the University of Waterloo, performing computational modelling and analysis as a member of the Computational Neuroscience Research Group (CNRG)---part of the broader Centre for Theoretical Neuroscience (CTN). I earned by Master of Applied Science in Systems Design Engineering as well as a Graduate Diploma in Theoretical Neuroscience from the University of Waterloo, during which I completed my Master's Thesis, ['A Biologically Plausible, Dynamics-based Model of Memory Using Neurosymbolic Methods'](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/2b4320d6-9c83-4164-917b-94cec9377901), led a student design team on research related to Brain-Computer-Interface spike decoding, and published a variety of research on spiking neural models and neurosymbolic methods. Throughout my studies, I served as a Teaching Assistant for a variety of courses: Computational Neuroscience (SYDE552), Dynamics (BME182), Linear Systems & Signals (BME252), and Pattern Recognition (SYDE675). I've previously worked as an Undergraduate Research Assistant with the Distributed Algorithms and Systems (DAS) Lab as well as the Vision and Image Processing (VIP) Lab at the University of Waterloo. Previously, I have worked as a Software Development Intern at The MathWorks, Inc. and an Engineering Intern at both Skyjack, Inc. and the Henny Penny Corp.. 

I am currently exploring PhD opportunities, focusing on theoretical and computational neuroscience. I am most interested in how we might bridge the gap between symbolic and connectionist understandings of intelligence, particularly as it concerns learning conceptual representations from multi-modal experiences. More commonly known as systems consolidation in the field of Neuroscience, replay of experiences held in memory play an essential role in the formation of concepts. I find this approach interesting due to its lack of emphasis on language; while language is important for communicating ideas, the formation of ideas, knowledge of categories, and ability to think all exist in absence of language. With a-linguistic thought, concept formation must, first-and-foremost, come from experience rather than, say, parsing the entirety of all human-generated text. Previous research has shown increases to model efficiency in learning when leveraging distributed, symbol-like representations under a vector-symbolic algebra. I hope to extend these methods and to develop models to leverage these methods.
